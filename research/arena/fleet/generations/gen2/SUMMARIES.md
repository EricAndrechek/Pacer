# Generation 2: what each agent did

One paragraph per agent, from its own hand-back notes. Scores are relative; absolute figures are left out because they come from one person's usage.

## g2-01-haiku

Claude model `claude-haiku-4-5`, effort high, niche weekly. Models: `g2-01-haiku-timestral-combo`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-209m`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-209m-extended`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-209m-mega`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-209m-optimal`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-28m`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-base`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-blend`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-context-1008`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-context-1152`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-context-1296`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-context-1344`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-context-144`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-context-336`, `g2-01-haiku-weekly-ewa`

Weekly meter. Focused on seven-day by tuning the Timestral family rather than building new methods: model size, context length, and a few blends. On four to eight visible episodes the 209M model with a 14-day context was about 8% better than the 123M baseline, with larger models and longer context helping up to a point and 21 days of history hurting. Blends and a weighted average of earlier combiners did slightly worse than the best configuration, and a three-day context was much worse. The agent concluded that simple configuration tuning beat complex ensembling, but the sample was tiny and the report gives no leaderboard-tier result. It noted that a spend-to-meter conversion helper would be needed for hybrid models and that the infrastructure worked without trouble. Most of its fifteen models are copies of the foundation-model meter children with different context settings.

## g2-02-sonnet

Claude model `claude-sonnet-4-6`, effort high, niche deep-ensemble. Models: `g2-02-sonnet-deep-meter`, `g2-02-sonnet-deep-meter-v5`, `g2-02-sonnet-deep-meter-v6`, `g2-02-sonnet-deep-meter-v7`, `g2-02-sonnet-deep-meter-v8`

Deep ensemble. Analysed the generation-1 board and concluded that the calibrated 123M wrapper was unbeatable on five-hour and seven-day and spend-decay-1d was unbeatable on scoped, and that mixtures only diluted the best child. Adaptive stacking picked a wider child on the visible tier because most visible episodes are low-usage ones, which hurt on the hidden tier, so later versions used fixed weights and finally pure routing: calibrated wrapper for five-hour and seven-day, spend-decay-1d for scoped. Early versions failed on ChildError and a confusing analog child. All three leaderboard calls timed out (a killed call, and two scorer timeouts caused by neural children running without a GPU), so the final router never received a score. Visible results were dominated by extreme low-usage episodes and the agent regarded them as unrepresentative. It judged the router as safe by construction (never worse than the best single child per target), without evidence beyond the earlier board.

## g2-03-opus

Claude model `claude-opus-4-6`, effort high, niche spend. Models: `g2-03-opus-spend-ens`, `g2-03-opus-spend-ens3`, `g2-03-opus-spend-fast`, `g2-03-opus-spend-hybrid`, `g2-03-opus-spend-stat`, `g2-03-opus-spend-stat2`

Spend and tokens. Built statistical models for hourly spend and tokens: weighted empirical quantiles (hour-of-day, day-of-week and recency kernels) with AR(1) copula paths, analog trajectory sampling, and surprise paths that stretch a share of paths for tail coverage. Path mixing beat quantile averaging or Schaake shuffling, and parametric normals were too narrow for zero-inflated, heavy-tailed spend. The fast hybrid (half quantile paths, half analog paths) beat the foundation models by about 30% on the visible tier but lost on the hidden tier; tail coverage was the weakness. The first statistical model had thin upper tails (top PIT bin about twice nominal) and a best-of-all variogram, and adding surprise paths improved CRPS by about 19% and fixed the tail. The later fast version was reported to beat the foundation model on the leaderboard in one call, while ensembles with children timed out. The agent filed upstream suggestions on the tail extension (a Pareto option), zero-inflated paths and Student-t innovations.

## g2-04-opus

Claude model `claude-opus-5`, effort high, niche fable-cap. Models: `g2-04-opus-fable-burst`, `g2-04-opus-fable-mix`, `g2-04-opus-fable-pool`

Fable cap. Studied the Fable weekly cap and found the median outcome is no movement at all, with a heavy right tail, so an extrapolating meter model cannot produce the right shape. The burst model resamples the account's own capped-family spend series with kernels on hour, weekday, recency, recent burn and time since the last capped-family turn (the most informative feature), converts spend to points with a random conversion rate, damps near the cap, outputs whole points and keeps a share of pure-persistence paths. An oracle experiment (true future spend known) cut CRPS only modestly, so most of the remaining error is the spend-to-points conversion, which varies by cycle and decays within a cycle. A re-base of the allowance after a model launch hit a couple of cycles and dominated aggregate scores; it was left unmodelled. Pooling with spend-decay and randomised rounding to whole points improved CRPS by a few percent and the limit calls by about a third; the best pool timed out on the scorer twice, so a mix variant that folds the decay paths into one process was submitted. On the hidden tier the mix gave far better limit calls and flatter PIT than the burst model alone, and the agent noted that the sandbox forbade parallel runs, so it replayed episodes offline.

## g2-05-sonnet

Claude model `claude-sonnet-5-5`, effort high, niche calibration. Models: `g2-05-sonnet-ens`, `g2-05-sonnet-final`, `g2-05-sonnet-flat`, `g2-05-sonnet-flat-kw`, `g2-05-sonnet-hazard`, `g2-05-sonnet-hazstack`, `g2-05-sonnet-l08`, `g2-05-sonnet-recal`, `g2-05-sonnet-stack`

Calibration. Found that the dominant miscalibration of the foundation-model meters is not width but missing probability mass at the current reading: the meter is an integer and usually unchanged, so truth falls below the whole continuous forecast. The flat model estimates the probability that the meter stays unchanged from the meter's own recent history (kernels on recency, time since last rise and recent rise), places that mass at the current level, keeps the child's quantiles for the moving part, mixes the 123M Timestral half and half with the six-hour decay model on weekly meters, and widens the upper tail. It turned skewed PIT histograms into nearly flat ones on the visible and leaderboard tiers and changed CRPS by only a few percent; fitted global shifts, backtest recalibration, five-hour median pinning and heavier ensembles did not help or timed out. The agent argued that the reported cover metrics count a degenerate interval as covered, so PIT is the honest read-out. Several leaderboard calls timed out with two or three foundation-model containers, so the submission uses one process. It also noted that CRPS varied a lot between near-identical models across calls because each call draws new episodes.

## g2-06-opus

Claude model `claude-opus-5-5`, effort high, niche wildcard. Models: `g2-06-opus-fmgate`, `g2-06-opus-fmgate2`

Wildcard. Gated the leaders by account state: when an account has been idle long enough, or a window sits at its limit until a later reset, paths hold at the current reading until a resume time sampled from the account's past idle stretches, then follow the base model's rise. It copied the best foundation-model meter model and the best split, next-start and spend models in-process, shrank the spread slightly, and rounded paths to whole points. Offline on visible episodes the gate cut five-hour CRPS by about a quarter on dormant accounts and scoped by about a tenth, and rounding fixed the five-hour PIT pile-up in the lowest bin. A first version that called the leaders as children timed out because stateful children are re-fitted at every checkpoint. A mid-cycle re-base hazard did not pay off and was left off. Hidden-tier calls drew very different episodes, so scores were noisy; calibration was the cleaner signal. The agent stopped early with most of the eval budget unused, saying cross-call comparisons were mostly noise, and asked for a mode that scores two models on the same hidden episodes. It also noted that the sandbox blocked process-pool parallelism.

## g2-07-opus

Claude model `claude-opus-5-5`, effort xhigh, niche limit-calls. Models: `g2-07-opus-limit`, `g2-07-opus-pool`

Limit calls. Built a simulator for hitting limits: a block bootstrap of the meter's own burn with a per-path intensity factor, a per-path ceiling (where the user stops) drawn from a survival-style estimate over past cycle tops, conditioned on how long the meter has sat at its current level, a learned late-cycle leak, coupling so that a blocked account holds its other meters, and a re-base hazard. The central finding was that weekly meters often park just under the limit rather than crossing it, and a per-hour slowdown near the top is the wrong shape because blasting through and parking are separate modes decided once. The ceilings-plus-parking version won on seven-day Brier and time-to-limit on the visible tier, but hits are rare there and a flat forecast is hard to beat. The submission pools the simulator with the small Timestral meter model in fixed shares (a TimesFM pool timed out); it ran end to end on the scorer with Brier competitive at seven-day but poor time-to-limit and over-predicted scoped rises. Leaderboard draws varied widely between calls. The agent left Toto untested because it could not run locally, and proposed learning pool weights online.

## g2-08-fable

Claude model `claude-fable-5-1`, effort high, niche five-hour. Models: `g2-08-fable-burst`, `g2-08-fable-burst-mix`, `g2-08-fable-burst-mix3`, `g2-08-fable-ref-timestral`

Five-hour. Modelled the five-hour meter as a burst process: a semi-Markov chain for active and idle five-minute bins using hazards by state age and local hour from the account's turn log, a gamma-distributed per-bin burn at a rate anchored on the recent rate, integer paths started from the latest reading plus converted spend, and 70% analog resampling of activity. It beat the Timestral baseline on visible A1-heavy episodes by about 15%, gave flat PIT and the best next-reading error, but lost on A2, where the hidden period is concentrated and dynamics are harder. The agent found that the half-window cycle rule merges two cycles when an allowance is re-based mid-window and handled it by taking the trailing run of readings. The submission mixes burst paths half and half with the 123M Timestral; on the hidden tier it roughly tied the Timestral reference on CRPS and was better on the other metrics, including limit calls and path shape. A three-way mix with Chronos2 timed out, and one call was lost to the one-hour foreground limit. Many tweaks (wider spread, frailty factors, a global rate multiplier) did not help.
